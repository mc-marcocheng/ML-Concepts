<TLDR>
Gradients can explode or vanish when backpropagation multiplies many Jacobians. Products with large singular values grow, while products with small singular values shrink.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Backpropagation applies the chain rule through many layers or time steps.

For hidden states:

$$
h_t = f(h_{t-1}, x_t),
$$

a gradient can contain products like:

$$
\frac{\partial h_T}{\partial h_t}
=
\prod_{k=t+1}^{T}
\frac{\partial h_k}{\partial h_{k-1}}.
$$

If the Jacobian norms are usually greater than one, gradients can explode.

If they are usually less than one, gradients can vanish.

This problem is common in deep networks and RNNs.

## Effects

Exploding gradients can cause unstable loss and `NaN` values.

Vanishing gradients can make early layers learn very slowly.

Saturating activation functions can make vanishing gradients worse.

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/activation-functions">Activation Functions</a>.

## Fixes

Use careful <a href="/learn/general-ml/weight-initialisation">Weight Initialization</a>.

Use residual connections and normalization layers.

Use gated architectures such as LSTMs.

Use <a href="/learn/applied-ml/gradient-clipping">Gradient Clipping</a> for exploding gradients.

Use non-saturating activations when suitable.

<Proof title="Why repeated factors shrink or grow">
Assume each scalar derivative has magnitude $a$.

A chain of $T$ such factors has magnitude:

$$
|a|^T.
$$

If $|a| < 1$, then $|a|^T$ moves toward zero as $T$ grows.

If $|a| > 1$, then $|a|^T$ grows as $T$ grows.

Matrix Jacobian products behave similarly through singular values.
</Proof>

## Common mistakes

Do not treat exploding and vanishing gradients as the same failure.

Do not assume clipping fixes vanishing gradients.

Do not ignore initialization and normalization.
