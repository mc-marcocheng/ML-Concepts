<TLDR>
The diffusion forward process adds Gaussian noise to data over many steps. A useful closed form lets us sample any noisy time step directly from clean data.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

A diffusion model defines a noising process from data to noise.

The forward process is fixed and does not learn parameters.

It produces a sequence:

$$
x_0, x_1, \dots, x_T.
$$

Here, $x_0$ is data and $x_T$ is close to standard Gaussian noise.

The learned model later reverses this process.

See <a href="/learn/generative-modeling/diffusion-reverse-process">Diffusion Reverse Process</a> for the sampling step.

## Markov noising chain

In DDPMs, each forward step adds Gaussian noise.

$$
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1})
=
\mathcal{N}
\left(
\sqrt{1-\beta_t}x_{t-1},
\beta_t I
\right).
$$

Define:

$$
\alpha_t = 1-\beta_t.
$$

Then:

$$
q(x_t \mid x_{t-1})
=
\mathcal{N}
\left(
\sqrt{\alpha_t}x_{t-1},
(1-\alpha_t)I
\right).
$$

The values $\beta_t$ form the noise schedule.

Small $\beta_t$ values make each step only slightly noisier.

## Closed form sampling

Define:

$$
\bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}\alpha_s.
$$

Then:

$$
q(x_t \mid x_0)
=
\mathcal{N}
\left(
\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0,
(1-\bar\alpha_t)I
\right).
$$

Thus, we can sample $x_t$ directly:

$$
x_t
=
\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0
+
\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon,
\quad
\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I).
$$

This avoids running all previous noising steps during training.

<Proof title="Closed form by induction">
For $t=1$, the formula matches the forward transition.

Assume:

$$
x_{t-1}
=
\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0
+
\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}}\epsilon_1.
$$

The next step is:

$$
x_t
=
\sqrt{\alpha_t}x_{t-1}
+
\sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\epsilon_2.
$$

Substitute the induction assumption:

$$
x_t
=
\sqrt{\alpha_t\bar\alpha_{t-1}}x_0
+
\sqrt{\alpha_t(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1})}\epsilon_1
+
\sqrt{1-\alpha_t}\epsilon_2.
$$

The two noise terms are independent Gaussians.

Their combined variance is:

$$
\alpha_t(1-\bar\alpha_{t-1}) + (1-\alpha_t)
=
1-\alpha_t\bar\alpha_{t-1}.
$$

Since $\bar\alpha_t=\alpha_t\bar\alpha_{t-1}$:

$$
x_t
=
\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0
+
\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\epsilon.
$$
</Proof>

## Noise prediction target

Training often samples a random time $t$.

Then it creates $x_t$ from $x_0$ and noise $\epsilon$.

A neural network learns to predict $\epsilon$.

$$
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathbb{E}_{x_0,t,\epsilon}
\left[
\|\epsilon-\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2
\right].
$$

This target also defines a score estimate.

See <a href="/learn/generative-modeling/score-function">Score Function</a> for the score view.

## Signal-to-noise ratio

The signal scale is $\bar\alpha_t$.

The noise scale is $1-\bar\alpha_t$.

A common signal-to-noise ratio is:

$$
\operatorname{SNR}(t)
=
\frac{\bar\alpha_t}{1-\bar\alpha_t}.
$$

Early time steps have high SNR.

Late time steps have low SNR.

## Code example

```python
import torch

def q_sample(x0, alpha_bar_t, noise=None):
    if noise is None:
        noise = torch.randn_like(x0)

    return (
        torch.sqrt(alpha_bar_t) * x0
        + torch.sqrt(1 - alpha_bar_t) * noise
    )
```

## Common mistakes

Do not learn the forward process in a standard DDPM.

Do not confuse $\alpha_t$ with $\bar\alpha_t`.

Do not sample all earlier steps when the closed form is available.
