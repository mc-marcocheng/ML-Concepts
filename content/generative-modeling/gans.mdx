<TLDR>
A GAN trains a generator to make fake samples and a discriminator to detect fake samples. At equilibrium, the generator distribution matches the data distribution.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

A Generative Adversarial Network has two networks.

The generator $G_\theta$ maps noise $z$ to a sample.

$$
x_{\text{fake}} = G_\theta(z), \quad z \sim p_z.
$$

The discriminator $D_\phi$ outputs the probability that a sample is real.

$$
D_\phi(x) \in (0,1).
$$

The generator defines a model distribution $p_g$.

Training tries to make $p_g$ close to the data distribution $p_{\text{data}}$.

GANs differ from <a href="/learn/generative-modeling/vae-elbo">VAEs</a> because they do not need an explicit likelihood.

They also differ from <a href="/learn/generative-modeling/diffusion-forward-process">diffusion models</a> because they generate in one forward pass.

## Minimax objective

The original GAN objective is:

$$
\min_G \max_D
\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p_{\text{data}}}[\log D(x)]
+
\mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}[\log(1-D(G(z)))].
$$

The discriminator maximizes correct classification.

The generator minimizes the discriminator's ability to detect fake samples.

This objective defines a two-player zero-sum game.

## Optimal discriminator

For a fixed generator, the best discriminator is:

$$
D^*(x)
=
\frac{p_{\text{data}}(x)}
{p_{\text{data}}(x)+p_g(x)}.
$$

If $p_g(x)=p_{\text{data}}(x)$, then:

$$
D^*(x)=\frac{1}{2}.
$$

The discriminator cannot do better than random guessing.

<Proof title="Optimal discriminator for a fixed generator">
For each value of $x$, the discriminator maximizes:

$$
p_{\text{data}}(x)\log D(x)
+
p_g(x)\log(1-D(x)).
$$

Let $a=p_{\text{data}}(x)$ and $b=p_g(x)$.

The objective for this $x$ is:

$$
a\log D + b\log(1-D).
$$

Differentiate with respect to $D$:

$$
\frac{a}{D} - \frac{b}{1-D}.
$$

Set the derivative to zero:

$$
\frac{a}{D}=\frac{b}{1-D}.
$$

Solve for $D$:

$$
a(1-D)=bD.
$$

Thus:

$$
a = (a+b)D.
$$

Therefore:

$$
D^*(x)=\frac{a}{a+b}
=
\frac{p_{\text{data}}(x)}
{p_{\text{data}}(x)+p_g(x)}.
$$
</Proof>

With this discriminator, the GAN objective relates to the Jensen-Shannon divergence.

The generator reaches its optimum when $p_g=p_{\text{data}}$.

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/jensen-shannon-divergence">Jensen-Shannon Divergence</a> for the divergence view.

## Non-saturating generator loss

The minimax generator loss can give small gradients early in training.

A common replacement is the non-saturating loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_G
=
-\mathbb{E}_{z \sim p_z}[\log D(G(z))].
$$

This loss asks the generator to make fake samples look real.

It gives stronger gradients when the discriminator is confident.

## Stability problems

GAN training can fail in several ways.

Mode collapse happens when the generator produces too few sample types.

Discriminator overpower happens when the discriminator becomes too accurate.

Oscillation can happen because each model changes the other's objective.

Common fixes include better architectures, spectral normalization, gradient penalties, and slower discriminator updates.

## Code example

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def gan_losses(discriminator, generator, real, latent_dim):
    batch_size = real.shape[0]
    device = real.device

    z = torch.randn(batch_size, latent_dim, device=device)
    fake = generator(z)

    real_logits = discriminator(real)
    fake_logits = discriminator(fake.detach())

    d_loss_real = F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(
        real_logits,
        torch.ones_like(real_logits),
    )
    d_loss_fake = F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(
        fake_logits,
        torch.zeros_like(fake_logits),
    )
    d_loss = d_loss_real + d_loss_fake

    fake_logits_for_g = discriminator(fake)
    g_loss = F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(
        fake_logits_for_g,
        torch.ones_like(fake_logits_for_g),
    )

    return d_loss, g_loss
```

Use `detach()` for fake samples in the discriminator loss.

This prevents discriminator updates from changing generator parameters.

## Common mistakes

Do not treat the discriminator as a fixed classifier.

Do not maximize the generator's original minimax loss when gradients saturate.

Do not ignore mode collapse when samples look sharp but lack diversity.
