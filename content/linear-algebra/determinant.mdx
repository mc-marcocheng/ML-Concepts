<TLDR>
The determinant of a square matrix measures signed volume scaling. A zero determinant means the matrix collapses space and is singular.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

For a square matrix $A$, the determinant is written $\det(A)$.

It tells how much $A$ scales signed volume.

If $\det(A)=0$, the map collapses space into a lower-dimensional set.

Then the matrix is singular.

See <a href="/learn/linear-algebra/singular-matrices">Singular Matrices</a>.

## Two by two formula

For:

$$
A=
\begin{bmatrix}
a & b \\
c & d
\end{bmatrix},
$$

the determinant is:

$$
\det(A)=ad-bc.
$$

This value is the signed area scale in two dimensions.

## Key properties

For square matrices $A$ and $B$:

$$
\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B).
$$

Also:

$$
\det(A^\top)=\det(A).
$$

If $A$ is invertible:

$$
\det(A^{-1})=\frac{1}{\det(A)}.
$$

See <a href="/learn/linear-algebra/inverse-of-a-matrix">Inverse of a Matrix</a>.

## Eigenvalue link

If $A$ has eigenvalues $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$, counted with multiplicity, then:

$$
\det(A)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_i.
$$

This connects determinant to <a href="/learn/linear-algebra/eigenvectors-eigenvalues">Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues</a>.

## Area proof for two dimensions

<Proof title="Two by two signed area">
The columns of $A$ are:

$$
u=(a,c)
$$

and

$$
v=(b,d).
$$

They form a parallelogram.

Its signed area is base times height with orientation.

Algebra gives:

$$
\det(A)=ad-bc.
$$

If one column is a scalar multiple of the other, the area is zero.

Thus, the determinant is zero for dependent columns.
</Proof>

## Code example

```python
import numpy as np

A = np.array([[2.0, 1.0],
              [3.0, 4.0]])

det = np.linalg.det(A)
print(det)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not use determinant for non-square matrices.

Do not use exact zero checks with floating point determinants.

Do not confuse determinant with rank.
