<TLDR>
The dot product turns two vectors into one scalar. It measures alignment, defines length, and gives projections.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

For vectors $a,b \in \mathbb{R}^n$, the dot product is:

$$
a^\top b
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i b_i.
$$

It is large and positive when vectors point in similar directions.

It is zero when vectors are perpendicular.

It is negative when vectors point in opposite directions.

See <a href="/learn/linear-algebra/orthogonality">Orthogonality</a> for the zero case.

## Length and angle

The dot product defines the Euclidean norm:

$$
\|a\|
=
\sqrt{a^\top a}.
$$

It also gives the angle $\theta$ between nonzero vectors:

$$
a^\top b
=
\|a\|\|b\|\cos \theta.
$$

This formula connects algebra to geometry.

## Projection

The projection of $a$ onto a nonzero vector $b$ is:

$$
\operatorname{proj}_b(a)
=
\frac{a^\top b}{b^\top b}b.
$$

The scalar coefficient is:

$$
\frac{a^\top b}{b^\top b}.
$$

Projection removes the part of $a$ that is orthogonal to $b$.

## Matrix view

A matrix-vector product computes many dot products at once.

If $A$ has rows $r_1,\dots,r_m$, then:

$$
Ax
=
\begin{bmatrix}
r_1^\top x \\
\vdots \\
r_m^\top x
\end{bmatrix}.
$$

This view helps with <a href="/learn/linear-algebra/jacobian">Jacobian</a> matrices and neural network layers.

## Code example

```python
import numpy as np

a = np.array([1.0, 2.0, 3.0])
b = np.array([4.0, 5.0, 6.0])

dot = a @ b
norm_a = np.sqrt(a @ a)

print(dot, norm_a)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not confuse a dot product with elementwise multiplication.

Do not use the angle formula with a zero vector.

Do not forget that $a^\top b$ is a scalar.
