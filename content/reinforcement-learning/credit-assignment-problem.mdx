<TLDR>
Credit assignment asks how much responsibility each earlier action has for later outcomes. Delayed rewards make this problem hard.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

An action can affect rewards many steps later.

The credit assignment problem asks which actions caused the reward.

This is hard when rewards are delayed, sparse, or noisy.

A good algorithm gives useful learning signals to important decisions.

## Temporal credit

Temporal credit assigns reward across time.

Monte Carlo returns assign the same future return to many past actions.

TD methods assign credit through bootstrapped value estimates.

Eligibility traces keep a decayed memory of visited states or actions.

GAE also mixes short and long credit paths.

See <a href="/learn/reinforcement-learning/gae">GAE</a>.

## Structural credit

Structural credit assigns reward across parts of a model or action.

For example, a multi-action policy may need to know which component mattered.

Neural networks use backpropagation for parameter credit.

RL adds the challenge that rewards can arrive after many decisions.

## Reward shaping

Reward shaping gives intermediate rewards.

It can speed learning.

It can also change the task if designed incorrectly.

Potential-based shaping can preserve the optimal policy.

## Common mistakes

Do not reward only the final outcome when useful intermediate signals exist.

Do not add shaping rewards that change the desired behavior.

Do not confuse noisy rewards with delayed rewards.
