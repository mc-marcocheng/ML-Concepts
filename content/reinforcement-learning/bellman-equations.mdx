<TLDR>
Bellman equations turn long-horizon return into a one-step recursion. They are the basis for value estimation, planning, and many reinforcement learning algorithms.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

The return satisfies:

$$
G_t = r_t + \gamma G_{t+1}.
$$

Taking conditional expectation gives a value recursion.

For a policy $\pi$:

$$
V^\pi(s)
=
\mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi, s' \sim P}
\left[
R(s,a)+\gamma V^\pi(s')
\right].
$$

The action value function satisfies:

$$
Q^\pi(s,a)
=
\mathbb{E}_{s' \sim P}
\left[
R(s,a)+\gamma
\mathbb{E}_{a' \sim \pi(\cdot \mid s')}
Q^\pi(s',a')
\right].
$$

## Optimal Bellman equation

The optimal value chooses the best action:

$$
V^*(s)
=
\max_a
\mathbb{E}_{s' \sim P}
\left[
R(s,a)+\gamma V^*(s')
\right].
$$

The optimal action value satisfies:

$$
Q^*(s,a)
=
\mathbb{E}_{s' \sim P}
\left[
R(s,a)+\gamma \max_{a'}Q^*(s',a')
\right].
$$

This equation appears in <a href="/learn/reinforcement-learning/q-learning-td-learning">Q-Learning and TD Learning</a>.

## Bellman backup

A Bellman backup replaces a value estimate with a one-step target.

For Q-learning, the target is:

$$
y = r+\gamma \max_{a'}Q(s',a').
$$

For SARSA, the target uses the next sampled action.

See <a href="/learn/reinforcement-learning/sarsa">SARSA</a> for the on-policy version.

## Proof idea

<Proof title="Bellman recursion from return">
Start with the discounted return:

$$
G_t=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^k r_{t+k}.
$$

Split the first reward from the rest:

$$
G_t
=
r_t+
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\gamma^k r_{t+k}.
$$

Factor out one $\gamma$:

$$
G_t
=
r_t+\gamma
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\gamma^j r_{t+1+j}.
$$

The remaining sum is $G_{t+1}$.

Thus:

$$
G_t=r_t+\gamma G_{t+1}.
$$

Now condition on $s_t=s$ and take expectation under policy $\pi$.

This gives:

$$
V^\pi(s)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[r_t+\gamma V^\pi(s_{t+1}) \mid s_t=s].
$$
</Proof>

## Common mistakes

Do not mix $V^\pi$ with $V^*$.

Do not use $\max$ in the policy evaluation equation.

Do not forget the expectation over transitions.
