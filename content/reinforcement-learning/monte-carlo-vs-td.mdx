<TLDR>
Monte Carlo methods wait for observed returns. TD methods update earlier by using current value estimates in the target.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Monte Carlo value estimation uses the sampled return:

$$
G_t=\sum_{k=0}^{T-t-1}\gamma^k r_{t+k}.
$$

A simple update is:

$$
V(s_t)
\leftarrow
V(s_t)+\alpha(G_t-V(s_t)).
$$

TD learning uses a one-step target:

$$
r_t+\gamma V(s_{t+1}).
$$

The update is:

$$
V(s_t)
\leftarrow
V(s_t)+\alpha(r_t+\gamma V(s_{t+1})-V(s_t)).
$$

## Bias and variance

Monte Carlo targets are unbiased samples of the true return.

They can have high variance.

TD targets use estimated values.

They can have bias early in learning.

They often have lower variance.

This tradeoff also appears in <a href="/learn/reinforcement-learning/gae">GAE</a>.

## Episode requirements

Monte Carlo methods need complete episodes.

TD methods can learn in continuing tasks.

TD methods can update after each step.

## When to use each

Use Monte Carlo when episodes are short and returns are easy to sample.

Use TD when rewards arrive slowly or tasks continue for a long time.

Many modern methods mix both views.

## Common mistakes

Do not say TD has no bias.

Do not say Monte Carlo always has lower error.

Do not use Monte Carlo returns before an episode ends.
