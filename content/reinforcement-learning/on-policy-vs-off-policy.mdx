<TLDR>
On-policy learning uses data from the policy being improved. Off-policy learning can use data from a different behavior policy.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

A behavior policy collects data.

A target policy is the policy being evaluated or improved.

In on-policy learning, the behavior policy and target policy are the same.

In off-policy learning, they can differ.

## Examples

SARSA is on-policy in its basic form.

Q-learning is off-policy because it learns a greedy target while behavior can explore.

PPO is usually on-policy because it reuses recent data only within a small policy change.

See <a href="/learn/reinforcement-learning/sarsa">SARSA</a> and <a href="/learn/reinforcement-learning/q-learning-td-learning">Q-Learning and TD Learning</a>.

## Why off-policy learning helps

Off-policy learning can use replay buffers.

It can learn from old data.

It can learn from demonstrations.

It usually needs corrections when the data policy differs from the target policy.

<a href="/learn/reinforcement-learning/importance-sampling">Importance Sampling</a> gives one correction method.

## Main tradeoffs

On-policy methods are simple and stable.

They often use data less efficiently.

Off-policy methods can use data better.

They can suffer from distribution mismatch and unstable targets.

## Common mistakes

Do not call any replay-buffer method off-policy without checking the update.

Do not ignore how old data differs from current policy data.

Do not use importance weights without variance control.
