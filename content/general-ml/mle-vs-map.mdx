<TLDR>
MLE finds parameters that make the data most likely. MAP also includes a prior over parameters.
</TLDR>

## Maximum likelihood

Given data $D$, maximum likelihood estimation solves:

$$
\theta_{\text{MLE}}
=
\arg\max_\theta p(D\mid \theta).
$$

It often uses the log likelihood:

$$
\theta_{\text{MLE}}
=
\arg\max_\theta \log p(D\mid \theta).
$$

Equivalently, minimize negative log likelihood.

## Maximum a posteriori

MAP estimation uses Bayes' theorem:

$$
p(\theta\mid D)
\propto
p(D\mid \theta)p(\theta).
$$

It solves:

$$
\theta_{\text{MAP}}
=
\arg\max_\theta
\left[
\log p(D\mid \theta)+\log p(\theta)
\right].
$$

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/bayes-theorem">Bayes' Theorem</a>.

## Link to regularization

The prior acts like a regularizer.

A Gaussian prior on weights gives an L2 penalty.

A Laplace prior gives an L1 penalty.

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/regularization-methods">Regularization Methods</a>.

## Example

Linear regression with Gaussian noise gives squared loss.

With a Gaussian prior on weights, MAP gives ridge regression.

## Common mistakes

Do not forget that MLE has no explicit prior.

Do not confuse MAP with full Bayesian inference.

Do not compare likelihood values across datasets of different size without care.
