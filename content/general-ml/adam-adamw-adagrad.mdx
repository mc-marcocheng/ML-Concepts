<TLDR>
Adaptive optimizers scale updates with gradient history. Adam uses first and second moments. AdamW decouples weight decay from Adam.
</TLDR>

## Adagrad

Adagrad accumulates squared gradients:

$$
G_t = G_{t-1}+g_t^2.
$$

The update is:

$$
\theta_{t+1}
=
\theta_t
-
\eta \frac{g_t}{\sqrt{G_t}+\epsilon}.
$$

Parameters with large past gradients get smaller steps.

## Adam

Adam tracks an exponential average of gradients:

$$
m_t=\beta_1m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)g_t.
$$

It also tracks squared gradients:

$$
v_t=\beta_2v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)g_t^2.
$$

With bias correction:

$$
\hat m_t=\frac{m_t}{1-\beta_1^t},
\quad
\hat v_t=\frac{v_t}{1-\beta_2^t}.
$$

The update is:

$$
\theta_{t+1}
=
\theta_t-\eta\frac{\hat m_t}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\epsilon}.
$$

## AdamW

AdamW decouples weight decay from the adaptive gradient step.

This often works better than L2 regularization inside Adam.

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/regularization-methods">Regularization Methods</a>.

## Code example

```python
m = beta1 * m + (1 - beta1) * g
v = beta2 * v + (1 - beta2) * (g * g)

m_hat = m / (1 - beta1 ** t)
v_hat = v / (1 - beta2 ** t)

theta = theta - lr * m_hat / (np.sqrt(v_hat) + eps)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not omit epsilon in the denominator.

Do not confuse AdamW with Adam plus L2 penalty.

Do not compare optimizers without learning-rate tuning.
