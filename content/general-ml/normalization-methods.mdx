<TLDR>
BatchNorm normalizes across a batch. LayerNorm normalizes within each example. RMSNorm normalizes by root mean square without mean subtraction.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Normalization layers control activation scale.

A common form is:

$$
\hat x=\frac{x-\mu}{\sqrt{\sigma^2+\epsilon}}.
$$

Then learned parameters scale and shift:

$$
y=\gamma \hat x+\beta.
$$

## BatchNorm

BatchNorm computes mean and variance across the batch.

It works well in many convolutional networks.

It uses running statistics at inference.

Small batches can make its statistics noisy.

## LayerNorm

LayerNorm computes statistics across features within one example.

It does not depend on other batch examples.

It is common in transformers and sequence models.

## RMSNorm

RMSNorm divides by the root mean square:

$$
\operatorname{RMS}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_i x_i^2+\epsilon}.
$$

It usually omits mean subtraction.

This can reduce compute in large models.

## Common mistakes

Do not use training BatchNorm statistics at inference.

Do not confuse BatchNorm and LayerNorm axes.

Do not remove epsilon from normalization.
