<TLDR>
Regularization adds constraints, penalties, or training rules that reduce overfitting. It often lowers variance at the cost of more bias.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Regularization changes training so the model does not only fit the training set.

A common objective is:

$$
L_{\text{total}}(\theta)
=
L_{\text{data}}(\theta)
+
\lambda R(\theta).
$$

The parameter $\lambda$ controls regularization strength.

## L2 regularization

L2 regularization penalizes squared weights:

$$
R(\theta)=\|\theta\|_2^2.
$$

It discourages large weights.

In linear models, it gives ridge regression.

## L1 regularization

L1 regularization penalizes absolute weights:

$$
R(\theta)=\|\theta\|_1.
$$

It can make weights exactly zero.

This helps with feature selection.

## Other methods

Common regularization methods include:

- early stopping
- dropout
- data augmentation
- weight decay
- label smoothing
- model averaging

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/early-stopping">Early Stopping</a>.

## Weight decay

Weight decay updates parameters toward zero during optimization.

For SGD, L2 regularization and weight decay can match.

For Adam, decoupled weight decay gives AdamW.

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/adam-adamw-adagrad">Adam, AdamW, and Adagrad</a>.

## Common mistakes

Do not tune regularization on the test set.

Do not assume all regularization reduces bias.

Do not confuse dropout at training with dropout at inference.
