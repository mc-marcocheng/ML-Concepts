<TLDR>
Jensen-Shannon divergence compares two distributions by measuring their KL divergence to the average distribution. It is symmetric and bounded.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Given two distributions $p$ and $q$, define their mixture:

$$
m = \frac{1}{2}(p+q).
$$

The Jensen-Shannon divergence is:

$$
D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p \Vert q)
=
\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert m)
+
\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert m).
$$

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/kl-divergence">KL Divergence</a> first.

## Symmetry

Jensen-Shannon divergence is symmetric.

$$
D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p \Vert q)
=
D_{\mathrm{JS}}(q \Vert p).
$$

This happens because the mixture $m$ does not change when you swap $p$ and $q$.

<Proof title="Symmetry of Jensen-Shannon divergence">
Define:

$$
m = \frac{1}{2}(p+q).
$$

If you swap $p$ and $q$, the mixture stays the same:

$$
\frac{1}{2}(q+p)=m.
$$

Now write the swapped divergence:

$$
D_{\mathrm{JS}}(q \Vert p)
=
\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert m)
+
\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert m).
$$

The two terms are the same terms in a different order.

Therefore:

$$
D_{\mathrm{JS}}(q \Vert p)
=
D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p \Vert q).
$$
</Proof>

## Entropy form

Jensen-Shannon divergence also has an entropy form.

$$
D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p \Vert q)
=
H(m) - \frac{1}{2}H(p) - \frac{1}{2}H(q).
$$

This form shows that Jensen-Shannon divergence measures the entropy increase from mixing.

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/entropy">Entropy</a> for the entropy definition.

## Bounds

With log base $2$:

$$
0 \le D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p \Vert q) \le 1.
$$

With natural logs:

$$
0 \le D_{\mathrm{JS}}(p \Vert q) \le \log 2.
$$

The lower bound occurs when $p=q$.

The upper bound occurs when the two distributions have disjoint support.

## Code example

```python
import numpy as np

def kl(p, q):
    mask = p > 0
    return np.sum(p[mask] * (np.log(p[mask]) - np.log(q[mask])))

p = np.array([0.5, 0.5, 0.0])
q = np.array([0.0, 0.5, 0.5])
m = 0.5 * (p + q)

js = 0.5 * kl(p, m) + 0.5 * kl(q, m)
print(js)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not confuse Jensen-Shannon divergence with KL divergence.

Do not forget the mixture distribution.

Do not forget that the bound depends on the log base.
