<TLDR>
A PMF gives probabilities for discrete values. A PDF gives density for continuous values. Areas under a PDF give probabilities.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

A probability mass function, or PMF, applies to discrete random variables.

$$
p(x) = \Pr(X = x).
$$

A probability density function, or PDF, applies to continuous random variables.

$$
\Pr(a \le X \le b)
=
\int_a^b p(x)\,dx.
$$

For a continuous variable, $p(x)$ is a density, not a probability.

## PMF rules

A valid PMF must satisfy two rules.

$$
p(x) \ge 0
$$

and

$$
\sum_x p(x) = 1.
$$

Example:

| $x$ | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $p(x)$ | 0.2 | 0.5 | 0.3 |

This PMF sums to one.

## PDF rules

A valid PDF must satisfy two rules.

$$
p(x) \ge 0
$$

and

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} p(x)\,dx = 1.
$$

A PDF can be greater than one. Only an area under the PDF gives a probability.

For example, the uniform PDF on $[0, 0.5]$ has density $2$.

$$
p(x)=2
\quad
\text{for}
\quad
0 \le x \le 0.5.
$$

Its total area is still one.

## CDF

The cumulative distribution function, or CDF, gives probability up to a value.

$$
F(x) = \Pr(X \le x).
$$

For a continuous random variable:

$$
F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^{x} p(t)\,dt.
$$

When the derivative exists:

$$
p(x) = \frac{d}{dx}F(x).
$$

## Link to expectation

Use the PMF or PDF to compute expectations.

For a discrete variable:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_x x p(x).
$$

For a continuous variable:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = \int x p(x)\,dx.
$$

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/expectation">Expectation</a> for more.

## Code example

```python
import numpy as np

x = np.array([0, 1, 2])
p = np.array([0.2, 0.5, 0.3])

is_valid_pmf = np.all(p >= 0) and np.isclose(np.sum(p), 1.0)
print(is_valid_pmf)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not treat a PDF value as a probability.

Do not forget that a PMF must sum to one.

Do not forget that a PDF must integrate to one.
