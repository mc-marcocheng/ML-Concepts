<TLDR>
Expectation gives the long-run average value of a random variable. It weights each value by its probability or density.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

The expectation of a random variable is its probability-weighted average.

For a discrete random variable $X$ with PMF $p(x)$:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_x x p(x).
$$

For a continuous random variable $X$ with PDF $p(x)$:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X] = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x p(x) \, dx.
$$

Use <a href="/learn/general-ml/pdf-pmf">PDF and PMF</a> to define the probability rule first.

## Expectation of a function

You often need the expectation of $g(X)$, not only $X$.

For a discrete random variable:

$$
\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \sum_x g(x)p(x).
$$

For a continuous random variable:

$$
\mathbb{E}[g(X)] = \int g(x)p(x)\,dx.
$$

This result is sometimes called the law of the unconscious statistician.

## Linearity

Expectation is linear.

$$
\mathbb{E}[aX + bY + c]
=
a\mathbb{E}[X] + b\mathbb{E}[Y] + c.
$$

This holds even when $X$ and $Y$ are dependent.

<Proof title="Linearity of expectation">
For discrete variables:

$$
\mathbb{E}[aX + bY + c]
=
\sum_x \sum_y (ax + by + c)p(x,y).
$$

Split the sum:

$$
=
a\sum_x \sum_y xp(x,y)
+
b\sum_x \sum_y yp(x,y)
+
c\sum_x \sum_y p(x,y).
$$

Now use the marginal probabilities:

$$
\sum_y p(x,y) = p(x),
\quad
\sum_x p(x,y) = p(y).
$$

Also:

$$
\sum_x \sum_y p(x,y) = 1.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\mathbb{E}[aX + bY + c]
=
a\mathbb{E}[X] + b\mathbb{E}[Y] + c.
$$
</Proof>

## Conditional expectation

A conditional expectation averages $X$ under a condition.

$$
\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y = y]
=
\sum_x x p(x \mid y).
$$

It is itself a function of $y$.

A useful identity is the law of total expectation:

$$
\mathbb{E}[X]
=
\mathbb{E}_Y[\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]].
$$

<Proof title="Law of total expectation">
For discrete variables:

$$
\mathbb{E}_Y[\mathbb{E}[X \mid Y]]
=
\sum_y \left(\sum_x x p(x \mid y)\right)p(y).
$$

Use the product rule:

$$
p(x \mid y)p(y) = p(x,y).
$$

Then:

$$
=
\sum_y \sum_x x p(x,y)
=
\sum_x x \sum_y p(x,y)
=
\sum_x x p(x)
=
\mathbb{E}[X].
$$
</Proof>

## Relation to variance

Variance measures spread around the expectation.

$$
\operatorname{Var}(X)
=
\mathbb{E}[(X - \mathbb{E}[X])^2].
$$

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/variance-and-covariance">Variance and Covariance</a> for this second moment.

## Code example

```python
import numpy as np

values = np.array([0, 1, 2, 3])
probs = np.array([0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.3])

expected_value = np.sum(values * probs)
print(expected_value)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not confuse the most likely value with the expected value.

Do not assume the expectation must be a possible value.

Do not require independence for linearity of expectation.
