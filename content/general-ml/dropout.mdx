<TLDR>
Dropout randomly sets activations to zero during training. At inference, use the full network with the correct scaling rule.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Dropout is a regularization method for neural networks.

During training, it samples a binary mask:

$$
m_i \sim \operatorname{Bernoulli}(1-p).
$$

Then it applies the mask to activations.

With inverted dropout:

$$
\tilde h_i=\frac{m_i h_i}{1-p}.
$$

Here $p$ is the dropout probability.

## Why it helps

Dropout makes units less dependent on specific other units.

It adds noise during training.

This can reduce overfitting.

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/regularization-methods">Regularization Methods</a>.

## Training and inference

Dropout is active during training.

Dropout is disabled during inference.

Inverted dropout scales activations during training, so no extra inference scaling is needed.

## Relation to ensembles

Dropout can be viewed as training many thinned networks that share weights.

The final network roughly averages these subnetworks.

This view links dropout to <a href="/learn/general-ml/ensembles">Ensembles</a>.

## Code example

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

dropout = nn.Dropout(p=0.2)

model.train()
h_train = dropout(h)

model.eval()
h_eval = dropout(h)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not leave dropout active during deterministic inference.

Do not add dropout everywhere by default.

Do not forget that dropout changes activation scale during training.
```
