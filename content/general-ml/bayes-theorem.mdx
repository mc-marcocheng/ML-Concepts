<TLDR>
Bayes' theorem converts a prior and a likelihood into a posterior. It gives a precise rule for belief updates under uncertainty.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Bayes' theorem states:

$$
p(A \mid B)
=
\frac{p(B \mid A)p(A)}{p(B)}.
$$

The terms have common names.

| Term | Name |
|---|---|
| $p(A)$ | prior |
| $p(B \mid A)$ | likelihood |
| $p(B)$ | evidence |
| $p(A \mid B)$ | posterior |

The posterior is proportional to likelihood times prior.

$$
p(A \mid B) \propto p(B \mid A)p(A).
$$

## Derivation

Bayes' theorem comes from the product rule.

$$
p(A,B)=p(A \mid B)p(B)
$$

and

$$
p(A,B)=p(B \mid A)p(A).
$$

Equate the two expressions:

$$
p(A \mid B)p(B)
=
p(B \mid A)p(A).
$$

Then divide by $p(B)$.

<Proof title="Bayes' theorem from the product rule">
The product rule gives:

$$
p(A,B)=p(A \mid B)p(B).
$$

It also gives:

$$
p(A,B)=p(B \mid A)p(A).
$$

Both expressions equal the same joint probability.

Therefore:

$$
p(A \mid B)p(B)=p(B \mid A)p(A).
$$

When $p(B)>0$:

$$
p(A \mid B)=\frac{p(B \mid A)p(A)}{p(B)}.
$$
</Proof>

## Evidence as normalization

For a set of mutually exclusive cases $A_1,\dots,A_k$:

$$
p(B)
=
\sum_i p(B \mid A_i)p(A_i).
$$

This makes the posterior probabilities sum to one.

For continuous parameters $\theta$:

$$
p(\theta \mid x)
=
\frac{p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta)}
{\int p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta)\,d\theta}.
$$

Use <a href="/learn/general-ml/pdf-pmf">PDF and PMF</a> to track discrete and continuous cases.

## Base rates

Base rates can strongly affect the posterior.

A rare condition can still have low posterior probability after a positive test.

This happens when false positives are not rare enough.

## MAP estimation

Maximum a posteriori estimation chooses the parameter with largest posterior.

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}}
=
\arg\max_\theta p(\theta \mid x).
$$

Since the evidence does not depend on $\theta$:

$$
\theta_{\mathrm{MAP}}
=
\arg\max_\theta p(x \mid \theta)p(\theta).
$$

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/mle-vs-map">MLE vs MAP</a> for the link to optimization.

## Code example

```python
prior = 0.01
sensitivity = 0.99
false_positive_rate = 0.05

evidence = sensitivity * prior + false_positive_rate * (1 - prior)
posterior = sensitivity * prior / evidence

print(posterior)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not ignore the prior.

Do not confuse $p(A \mid B)$ with $p(B \mid A)$.

Do not forget the evidence term.
