<TLDR>
A confidence interval is a range made by a procedure with a stated long-run coverage rate. It does not assign probability to a fixed parameter.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

A confidence interval estimates an unknown population parameter.

A common form is:

$$
\text{estimate} \pm \text{critical value} \times \text{standard error}.
$$

The standard error measures the sampling variation of the estimate.

For a sample mean with known standard deviation $\sigma$:

$$
\bar{x} \pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}.
$$

## Meaning of 95 percent confidence

A 95 percent confidence interval comes from a procedure.

If you repeat the full sampling procedure many times, about 95 percent of the intervals contain the true parameter.

The parameter is fixed in the frequentist view.

The interval is random because it depends on the sample.

## Unknown variance

Usually, the population standard deviation is unknown.

Then use the sample standard deviation $s$ and a $t$ critical value:

$$
\bar{x} \pm t_{\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}.
$$

The $t$ distribution has heavier tails than the normal distribution.

This accounts for uncertainty from estimating the variance.

## Standard error

For independent samples with variance $\sigma^2$, the sample mean has variance:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X})
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
$$

Thus, the standard error is:

$$
\operatorname{SE}(\bar{X})
=
\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}.
$$

<Proof title="Variance of the sample mean">
Let:

$$
\bar{X}
=
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i.
$$

Assume the $X_i$ are independent and each has variance $\sigma^2$.

Then:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\bar{X})
=
\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i\right).
$$

Pull out the constant:

$$
=
\frac{1}{n^2}\operatorname{Var}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_i\right).
$$

Independence lets variances add:

$$
=
\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Var}(X_i).
$$

Substitute $\sigma^2$:

$$
=
\frac{1}{n^2}n\sigma^2
=
\frac{\sigma^2}{n}.
$$
</Proof>

## Bootstrap intervals

A bootstrap interval estimates uncertainty by resampling the observed data.

A simple percentile bootstrap works as follows.

1. Sample $n$ points with replacement from the data.

2. Compute the statistic.

3. Repeat many times.

4. Use quantiles of the bootstrap statistics.

Bootstrap methods help when analytic standard errors are hard.

## Code example

```python
import numpy as np
from scipy import stats

x = np.array([1.2, 0.9, 1.4, 1.1, 1.0])
n = len(x)

mean = np.mean(x)
se = stats.sem(x)
crit = stats.t.ppf(0.975, df=n - 1)

lo = mean - crit * se
hi = mean + crit * se

print(lo, hi)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not say there is a 95 percent probability that this fixed interval contains the fixed parameter.

Do not ignore dependence between samples.

Do not confuse confidence intervals with prediction intervals.
