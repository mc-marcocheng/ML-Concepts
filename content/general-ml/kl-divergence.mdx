<TLDR>
KL divergence measures how much distribution $q$ differs from distribution $p$, when samples come from $p$. It is non-negative and asymmetric.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

For discrete distributions $p$ and $q$ over the same support:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q)
=
\sum_x p(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.
$$

Equivalently:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q)
=
\mathbb{E}_{x \sim p}
\left[
\log p(x) - \log q(x)
\right].
$$

The direction matters. In general:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q)
\ne
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p).
$$

## Relation to entropy

KL divergence equals cross-entropy minus entropy.

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q)
=
H(p,q)-H(p).
$$

This follows from:

$$
H(p,q)
=
-\sum_x p(x)\log q(x)
$$

and

$$
H(p)
=
-\sum_x p(x)\log p(x).
$$

See <a href="/learn/general-ml/entropy">Entropy</a> for these terms.

## Non-negativity

KL divergence is always non-negative.

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q) \ge 0.
$$

It equals zero only when $p=q$ almost everywhere.

<Proof title="Gibbs inequality">
Use the fact that $\log u \le u - 1$ for $u > 0$.

Let:

$$
u = \frac{q(x)}{p(x)}.
$$

Then:

$$
\log\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}
\le
\frac{q(x)}{p(x)} - 1.
$$

Multiply by $p(x)$:

$$
p(x)\log\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}
\le
q(x)-p(x).
$$

Sum over $x$:

$$
\sum_x p(x)\log\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}
\le
\sum_x q(x)-\sum_x p(x)
=
0.
$$

Multiply by $-1$:

$$
\sum_x p(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}
\ge
0.
$$

Therefore:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q) \ge 0.
$$
</Proof>

## Support mismatch

If $p(x)>0$ and $q(x)=0$ for some $x$, then:

$$
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q)=\infty.
$$

The model distribution $q$ assigns zero probability to an event that data distribution $p$ can generate.

## Mode covering and mode seeking

The two KL directions behave differently.

Minimizing $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \Vert q)$ often encourages $q$ to cover all modes of $p$.

Minimizing $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q \Vert p)$ often encourages $q$ to focus on one high-density mode.

This difference matters in approximate inference and generative models.

## Code example

```python
import numpy as np

p = np.array([0.5, 0.4, 0.1])
q = np.array([0.4, 0.4, 0.2])

kl = np.sum(p * (np.log(p) - np.log(q)))
print(kl)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not treat KL divergence as a distance metric.

Do not forget that KL divergence is asymmetric.

Do not allow zero values in $q$ where $p$ is positive.
