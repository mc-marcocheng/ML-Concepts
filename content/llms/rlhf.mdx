<TLDR>
RLHF uses preference labels to train a reward model. It then optimizes a language model to get high reward while staying near a reference model.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

RLHF means reinforcement learning from human feedback.

A common pipeline has three stages.

1. Train a base model with pretraining.

2. Supervised finetune it on good responses.

3. Optimize it with a reward model from human preferences.

The final stage often uses a KL penalty.

The penalty keeps the policy close to a reference model.

## Preference data

Annotators compare two or more responses to the same prompt.

The data says which response is preferred.

This gives pairwise preference examples.

A reward model learns to score responses.

## Reward model loss

For a preferred response $y_w$ and a rejected response $y_l$, a common loss is:

$$
\mathcal{L}
=
-\log \sigma(r_\phi(x,y_w)-r_\phi(x,y_l)).
$$

This trains the reward model to score the preferred response higher.

## Policy optimization

The policy model generates responses.

The reward model scores them.

The training objective often includes:

$$
\mathbb{E}[r_\phi(x,y)]
-
\beta \operatorname{KL}(\pi_\theta(\cdot|x)\|\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot|x)).
$$

The KL term reduces reward hacking and distribution drift.

## Common mistakes

Do not treat the reward model as ground truth.

Do not optimize reward without checking generated samples.

Do not remove the KL penalty without a reason.

Do not confuse RLHF with supervised finetuning.
