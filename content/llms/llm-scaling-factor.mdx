<TLDR>
Scaled dot-product attention divides logits by $\sqrt{d_k}$. This keeps logit variance controlled and helps prevent softmax saturation.
</TLDR>

## Core idea

Attention uses dot products between queries and keys.

For a query $q$ and key $k$:

$$
s = q^\top k.
$$

Scaled attention uses:

$$
s = \frac{q^\top k}{\sqrt{d_k}}.
$$

Here $d_k$ is the key dimension.

The scale appears inside <a href="/learn/llms/causal-attention">causal attention</a> and cross attention.

## Why scale by square root

Assume each component of $q$ and $k$ has mean zero and variance one.

Assume components are independent.

Then:

$$
q^\top k = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i.
$$

Each product has variance one.

The sum has variance $d_k$.

Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ gives variance near one.

<Proof title="Variance of the attention dot product">
Let:

$$
s = \sum_{i=1}^{d_k} q_i k_i.
$$

Assume $q_i$ and $k_i$ are independent.

Assume each has mean zero and variance one.

Then:

$$
\mathbb{E}[q_i k_i]=0.
$$

Also:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(q_i k_i)
=
\mathbb{E}[q_i^2 k_i^2]
=
\mathbb{E}[q_i^2]\mathbb{E}[k_i^2]
=
1.
$$

The terms are independent, so variances add:

$$
\operatorname{Var}(s)=d_k.
$$

For $s/\sqrt{d_k}$:

$$
\operatorname{Var}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)
=
\frac{d_k}{d_k}
=
1.
$$
</Proof>

## Softmax saturation

Large logits make softmax very sharp.

A sharp softmax can produce small gradients for most positions.

The scaling factor keeps logits in a safer range.

It does not solve all stability problems.

## Code example

```python
import torch
import torch.nn.functional as F

def scaled_attention_scores(q, k):
    d_k = q.shape[-1]
    return q @ k.transpose(-2, -1) / d_k**0.5

scores = scaled_attention_scores(q, k)
weights = F.softmax(scores, dim=-1)
```

## Common mistakes

Do not divide by $d_k$.

Do not apply the scale after softmax.

Do not confuse this with LLM scaling laws.

Use <a href="/learn/llms/scaling-laws">scaling laws</a> for model-size scaling.
